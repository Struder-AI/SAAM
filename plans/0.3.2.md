## Small, truthful map reads and complexity reduction

**CLI map reads — owner revised 2026-10-02:** one default supplies parent-map interactions and exact source ranges; agents read leaf code with normal file tools, never another map read. Remove `--source`, `--code` and `--details`; direct contract/link reads supply complete interfaces. Preserve the human viewer. The [map read contract](../dev-map/README.md#commands) owns mechanics and replacement requirements: integrations must carry the existing semantics rather than bypassing the presentation boundary. Keep orientation short and visible information complete; no truncation, hidden summaries or return to historical oversized reads.

**Complexity rule, owner direction:** a special-case branch, alternate producer, compatibility shim or repair pass is evidence that the operation/interface may be wrong. Before adding one, identify the common operation and the invariant that could make the exception unnecessary. For Slice, the accepted direction is one owned-region/reference-family operation; lip, cladding, skin, brim and support become inputs, not separate lifecycles. When a general operation replaces an old path, rewire every consumer and remove the old path, dispatch and stale guidance. Review the actual before/after call paths and owner boundaries, identify every surviving special-case path, and justify any irreducible physical exception with a concrete constraint. Closing a worker milestone requires the lead to verify the original user-visible outcome on its real entry path and the removal of superseded paths; parity, static map checks and narrower lifecycle tests establish only their stated properties. Passing tests, short functions or a map with fewer boxes do not by themselves establish reduced complexity. This is an implementation/review criterion across 0.3.2, not a proposal to add another wrapper or slogan.

Both desktop launches require first Send; Codex wakeup remains unverified.

Disposable installer/profile trials preserve unrelated settings and data.
