# Paath contract design and research

2026-10-20. Research and design rationale for the authored Paath type contract in
[the canonical map](../contracts/map.json). Read `@type/paath`, `@type/work` and
Construction map 3 before implementation. New /3 publication and canonical
admission are integrated for named small fixtures; full consumer, compatibility
and numerical qualification is unfinished. The [session audit](0.4.0-session-intent-audit.md)
records known omissions; this document is not a claim of completed migration.

## Geometry

Keep native NURBS degrees 1–3, plus explicit point geometry. Use the natural degree:
straight segments need degree 1, conics can use rational degree 2, and freeform
native/authored curves and bounded surface images use degree 3. Do not elevate
everything to cubic or add a compulsory degree-reduction pass.

Actual producers are [authored curves](../core/path/authored-curves.mjs),
[curve fitting](../core/geom/curve-fit.mjs),
[mapped surface paths](../core/geom/surface-path.mjs) and
[construction retention](../core/path/construction-curves.mjs).
A bounded synthetic comparison used one exact cubic versus same-parameter
approximations at 1 micrometre positional tolerance: 110 bytes versus about
6 KB of quadratic or 20 KB of linear NURBS. These counts are illustrative,
not optimal lower bounds. Evaluation timings were comparable; precise length
integration favored lower degree in that example. No whole-generation or saved
bundle corpus performance claim follows. Reducing mandatory material integration
is a separate simplification from removing cubic geometry.

## Moving material

Canonical anchors carry `r`, material per distance (mm3/mm = mm2), and
`a`, width/height. Under the rectangular heuristic:

```
width  = sqrt(r * a)
height = sqrt(r / a)
flow   = r * requested-or-realized-speed
```

Changing aspect preserves material per distance algebraically. At `r=.08`,
`a=2` gives `.4 x .2 mm`; `a=1` gives a `.28284 mm` square. A slowdown reduces
flow without changing section. Defining r as mm3/s instead couples section to
speed and fails at stationary motion; reserve that quantity for execution and
stationary deposition.

Interpolate the canonical r/a anchors, then derive dimensions. Linear h/w and
linear r/a are not interchangeable between matching endpoints. For example,
linear `.2→.4` height and `.4→.8` width give midpoint area `.18`; converting only
the endpoint areas and interpolating gives `.20`. Existing cumulative-volume
and dimension profiles therefore need explicit regeneration, a retained reader,
or a disclosed local-error approximation; never silently convert just their keys.

Integrated material-volume and realized machine-timing estimates are bonus
objectives, removed from the required 0.4.0 migration scope by the owner on
2026-10-20. A better heuristic can be added later. Local material commands remain
necessary; machine outputs derive emitted material from their actual output
segments. Evaluating or displaying a local bead requires no integrated totals.
An explicit stationary dose or dwell is an instruction. Existing minimal-effort
travel and cooling preferences remain contractual.

Slice resolves its requested width and actual local normal depth before
publication. A deeper gap at the same width increases material per distance and
changes aspect: `r=width*depth`, `a=width/depth`. It must not preserve rate by
silently narrowing the requested bead. A separately requested downstream aspect
change can preserve rate; that algebraic transformation is a different operation.
Depth greater than width produces shared printability advice, not a refusal when
the person wishes to try it. Final attributes, placement and spacing precede
support, coverage and clearance decisions. Paath positions name the bead top;
remaining centered Studio conventions must be reconciled with that same data.

Contact ovals are a separate, tunable heuristic derived from the rectangular
r/a dimensions. They do not redefine accounting dimensions, preserve oval area
as material volume, or add a volume-integration requirement. Each measured bead
pair and each bead's own tool pose determine independently oriented contact
frames, including off-kilter cases; global-Z alignment is insufficient. The
contact result and its viewer must use the same declared model and expose its
numerical limits. Oval placement, finite coverage and degeneracies still need a
coherent implementation decision; current rectangular contact is not oval
conformance merely because it uses the same r/a inputs.

## Direction and logical tools

Owner direction: Paath does not require intrinsic tool roll; adapters derive it.
Paath carries tool-axis direction and requested scalar speed. This
deliberately retires authored roll authority in current full-frame source poses;
adapters construct continuous feasible frames instead of recovering absent intent.

Material section orientation is separate. Existing code supports a changing bead
normal with fixed nozzle direction. Preserve sufficient deposition-reference
information to derive the section frame; use a section-normal anchor where it
cannot be derived. A nonsquare bead's orientation is not determined by XYZ+r/a.

A Paath tool index can identify a logical extrusion/material selection, mapped
by the bundle to material, process and eventual physical nozzle. Distinct logical
indexes may map to one nozzle and still require a material change. Avoid equating
logical identity with a hardware nozzle number.

## Points and order

Point Paath remains useful for stationary work before scheduling. Arrival and
departure travel do not replace an injection, dwell or in-place orientation
action. Material-per-distance cannot define a positive stationary dose. Keep
explicit action quantity/rate/duration semantics where required, rather than
overloading r with different units or inventing microscopic travel.

Owner clarification: Scheduler may split a Paath entity into new entities, each
with its own schedule index. This accommodates interleaving: A can become A1 and
A2, ordered around B. It applies to polylines, NURBS and connected multi-piece
curves. Splitting must preserve the curve and its anchor meaning, using an exact
trim or equivalent shared geometry view rather than a refit. New entities can
share underlying geometry where useful; a separate range-list format is not a
requirement. Keep stable entity identity separate from the order index so later
renumbering does not break prerequisites or inspection identities. Final indexes
can be assigned when the output order is complete.

Owner agrees that arrival/departure travel cannot replace the stationary action.
Point entities therefore retain injection/dwell semantics after scheduling.

Recommend Scheduler own deposition order, permitted traversal and collision-aware
travel between entities. Export owns machine-specific realization, service
actions, placement, kinematics and machine output. Current
[machine execution preparation](../core/export/transfer-planning.mjs) checks
captured nozzle assumptions and resolves physical transfers. Bundle invokes
Scheduler before review when actual geometry exceeds saved assumptions. Ordinary
adapter translation cannot conceal a changed deposition order.

Owner accepts the separation: extensions can produce Paath without nozzle
geometry, carrying neutral logical tool indexes. Scheduler receives a nozzle type
for each index (hole diameter and angle), using defaults when none are supplied.
Capture the resolved assumptions with the result. Machine assignment subsequently
maps those indexes to concrete machine toolheads and associated settings.

Paath remains machine-independent; a schedule states the envelopes for which it
is qualified. On machine assignment, reuse qualification if the actual
envelopes are contained and the other scheduling assumptions still hold;
otherwise ask Scheduler to check/recompute under actual constraints. Qualification
is scoped to the supplied nozzle cone (external flat-tip diameter and angle),
with declared orientation and forgiveness. Full carriage, parked-tool and fixture
collision modeling remains outside this 0.4.0 nozzle contract; existing future
intent stays in 0.5.0.
Final qualification precedes human Export approval. A changed
machine may invalidate the schedule qualification without invalidating source
geometry/material. If no ordering works for the selected tool, return the scoped
incompatibility rather than modifying deposition to fit it.

The logical-tool / assumed-nozzle / actual-toolhead separation is accepted owner
intent. Envelope qualification and final-review mechanics above are the proposed
implementation of that separation. Conservative universal envelopes can
unnecessarily reject valid parts, so they should not be a mandatory substitute
for actual tool assignment.
