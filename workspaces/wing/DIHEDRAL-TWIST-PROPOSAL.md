# Dihedral and twist proposal for 0.3.1

This is a design proposal, not an implemented or qualified control. The current Wing workspace prints separate span-up sections with an integrated flat-face winglet at each tip. Each section's hollow skin, webs and rod passage are one explicit continuous Trace route. With sweep, each half-wing has its own straight rod axis; there is no through-rod across the root.

- **Dihedral control:** propose 0–8° per half-wing. Positive angle raises the tip relative to the root in the assembled view. Each half's rod axes remain straight and rise with its wing. Section joints stay perpendicular to that half's span axis and mate within the half. Printing can keep the local span upright by rotating the entire section into its print frame; the outermost section still needs a flat outer winglet face for its bed contact.
- **Twist control:** propose −4 to +4° at the tip, linearly interpolated from zero at the root. Negative means leading edge down relative to the trailing edge (washout), measured about each station's quarter chord. Keep the rod axes fixed and straight while the skin rotates; compute each station's true bore intersection with those axes and reject stations where material or clearance is insufficient. Twist need not bend the rods.
- **Construction checks:** recompute envelope, rod intersections, flap pivot clearance, lofted geometry and the complete rising Trace route at every station. Reject a design with a named piece/station when a bore breaks through, a route loses correspondence or continuity, or the flat winglet bed face cannot be retained. Avoid silently moving rods or changing selected thermal settings. Save the chosen angles and source geometry with every new bundle set.

The root joint needs an explicit choice. A center saddle or fuselage mount with two angled sockets keeps each half's straight rod separate and gives a deliberate load path; this is the recommended direction, but it adds a new wing-related printable component and needs dimensions. Directly beveling and gluing the two half-wing roots keeps the part count down but leaves rod anchoring and joint strength unresolved. Neither choice establishes strength or flight safety. The owner must select a joint, rod insertion/retention method, and winglet flat-face treatment under combined dihedral and twist before these controls become manufacturing options. Alternate tip constructions and moving control surfaces need separate geometry and Trace proposals.
